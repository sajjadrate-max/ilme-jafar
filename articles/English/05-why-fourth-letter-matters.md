# Why the Fourth Letter Is Important

Anyone learning Ilm-e-Jafar Akhbar quickly encounters a recurring rule: after the Jafar grid is built and its letters recounted column by column, it is specifically every fourth letter that gets extracted to form the basis of the answer. This raises a natural question — why the fourth letter specifically, and not the third, fifth, or some other position? In this article, we explore the reasoning and tradition behind this specific choice.

## Is the Fourth Letter Always Used?

While "the fourth-letter method" is the most well-known and widely practiced form of Ilm-e-Jafar Akhbar, some traditional schools of thought describe variations using the third or fifth letter instead. This variation tends to depend on regional tradition and the teaching lineage of a particular practitioner. However, the choice of the number four is by far the most common and widely recognized, which is why most modern tools follow this same traditional approach.

## The Symbolic Significance of Four

In traditional spiritual thought, the number four holds a special place. Islamic tradition speaks of the four classical elements (fire, water, earth, and air), which were considered the foundation of the material world. Similarly, the four cardinal directions (east, west, north, south) represent a complete circle. The choice of four in Ilm-e-Jafar is thought to draw on this same idea of completeness and balance — that is, after four steps, a full cycle is completed, and the letter that emerges from that completed cycle is considered particularly meaningful.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What happens if there are fewer than four letters in the grid?**
In such cases, traditional practitioners handle it differently — some take the last available letter in the sequence, while others suggest lengthening the question slightly so the grid contains enough letters to work with properly.

**Are the letters other than the fourth completely useless?**
No, the remaining letters still play a role in the grid's overall structure, but tradition holds that only the sequence of every fourth letter carries the strongest spiritual relevance for the final answer.

**Is this rule derived from the Quran or Hadith?**
No, this is a centuries-old spiritual tradition attributed to the founders of Ilm-e-Jafar, not a direct instruction from the Quran or Hadith. It should be understood as a traditional, interpretive practice passed down through generations rather than a matter of religious obligation.

## Precautions When Using This Method

A few important points should be kept in mind when relying on the fourth-letter method. First, the question should be asked with a sincere heart and pure intention. Second, the same question should not be repeated across multiple sittings, as this is believed to affect the reliability of the outcome. Third, performing ablution and reciting Durood Shareef before beginning is considered a recommended practice. Fourth, and most importantly, one should always remember that ultimate knowledge belongs to Allah alone, and this method is simply a traditional means of guidance rather than a certain prediction of the future.

## Frequently Asked Questions (Continued)

**Does the fourth-letter rule apply differently for very long questions?**
The underlying rule remains the same regardless of question length — every fourth letter of the recounted sequence is extracted — but longer questions naturally produce more extracted letters, which can sometimes make the resulting interpretation more detailed and nuanced.

**Can this method be combined with other traditional practices?**
Yes, many practitioners use Ilm-e-Jafar Akhbar alongside istikhara or other forms of guidance, treating each as a complementary source of reflection rather than a standalone final verdict.

## The Number Four in Other Traditional Sciences

It's a fascinating observation that the number four holds special importance not only in Ilm-e-Jafar but in several other traditional fields of knowledge as well. Traditional Greek-influenced medicine (Tibb-e-Yunani) speaks of four bodily humors — blood, phlegm, yellow bile, and black bile. Islamic jurisprudence traditionally recognizes four major schools of thought (Hanafi, Shafi'i, Maliki, and Hanbali). Even classical astronomy referenced four cardinal points as fundamental reference markers. These parallels suggest that the choice of four in Ilm-e-Jafar reflects this same broader traditional intellectual current, in which the human body, spirit, and surrounding cosmos were often understood through frameworks rooted in the natural elements.

## A Practical Example

Suppose a question, once arranged in the Jafar grid and recounted column by column, produces a new sequence of sixteen letters. Following the fourth-letter rule, the practitioner would extract the letters occupying positions 4, 8, 12, and 16 in this sequence — four letters in total. These four letters are then combined, their abjad values summed, and the result cross-referenced against traditional interpretive meanings to produce the final answer, along with an appropriate wazifa suited to the situation.

## A Comparison With Other Number Choices

Understanding why four was chosen becomes clearer when we compare it briefly with other numbers used elsewhere in Ilm-e-Jafar Aasar. The number seven, for instance, is tied to the days of the week and traditional planetary associations, making it suited to timing-related questions. The number nine is associated with completion and transformation, useful for gauging spiritual states. The number twenty-eight corresponds to the full Arabic alphabet, offering the most detailed analysis available. Against this backdrop, four's association with foundational, elemental completeness makes it particularly well suited to Akhbar's purpose: identifying the essential nature of an answer quickly and directly, rather than exploring a wider or more nuanced dimension of a question.

## Final Thoughts

The choice of four is not arbitrary but reflects a deliberate, symbolically grounded tradition passed down through generations of practitioners. Understanding this reasoning helps deepen appreciation for the method beyond simply following its mechanical steps.

## Respecting the Tradition While Using Modern Tools

As Ilm-e-Jafar continues to move into digital spaces, it becomes even more important for users to understand the reasoning behind each rule, rather than treating the process as a purely mechanical button-click. Knowing why the fourth letter carries significance helps a user engage with the result thoughtfully, appreciating it as part of a living tradition rather than viewing it as an arbitrary output from an algorithm.

The selection of the fourth letter lies at the heart of the entire Ilm-e-Jafar Akhbar system. While this calculation is now completed instantly through modern tools, the centuries-old tradition and thinking behind it remain just as alive today as they were when this method first took shape, connecting today's users to a long, unbroken chain of practice that stretches back many generations.
