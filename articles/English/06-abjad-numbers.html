Abjad Numbers and Their Letters

At the heart of every method within Ilm-e-Jafar lies the abjad system — the traditional assignment of numerical values to the letters of the Arabic alphabet. Without understanding abjad, it is difficult to grasp the deeper workings of this practice. In this article, we take a detailed look at how the abjad system works and where it comes from.

What Is Abjad?

Abjad refers to an ancient ordering of the Arabic alphabet in which every letter carries a fixed numerical value. Unlike the modern alphabetical order used in dictionaries, the abjad order follows a specific traditional sequence, and each letter's position in that sequence determines its assigned number.

Abjad Kabir and Abjad Saghir

Traditional calculation uses two major systems: "Abjad Kabir" (the greater abjad) and "Abjad Saghir" (the lesser abjad). In Abjad Kabir, each letter retains its full original numerical value — for instance, Ghain (غ) equals 1000. In Abjad Saghir, larger values are reduced into smaller units by essentially removing trailing zeros (so 1000 becomes simply 1). Most traditional methods within Ilm-e-Jafar use Abjad Kabir, since it provides more detailed and precise results.

The Complete Abjad Table

The full traditional abjad table assigns the following values: Alif (ا) = 1, Ba (ب) = 2, Jeem (ج) = 3, Dal (د) = 4, Ha (ہ) = 5, Waw (و) = 6, Zay (ز) = 7, Ha (ح) = 8, Ta (ط) = 9, Ya (ی) = 10, Kaf (ک) = 20, Lam (ل) = 30, Meem (م) = 40, Noon (ن) = 50, Seen (س) = 60, Ain (ع) = 70, Fa (ف) = 80, Sad (ص) = 90, Qaf (ق) = 100, Ra (ر) = 200, Sheen (ش) = 300, Ta (ت) = 400, Tha (ث) = 500, Kha (خ) = 600, Dhal (ذ) = 700, Dad (ض) = 800, Za (ظ) = 900, and Ghain (غ) = 1000. This traditional order is often memorized through the mnemonic phrase "Abjad Hawwaz Hutti Kalaman Sa'fas Qarashat Thakhadh Dazagh," which encodes exactly this sequence of 28 letters into a memorable phrase.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do the additional letters used in Urdu (such as ٹ, ڈ, ڑ) have their own abjad value?

Since the abjad system is based purely on the Arabic alphabet, letters that exist in Urdu but not in Arabic generally don't have a separately defined traditional value. They are typically counted as equivalent to the closest matching Arabic letter.

Does the abjad value of a name always stay the same?

Yes, since each letter's value is fixed, the same name will always produce the same abjad total, regardless of who performs the calculation.

Do hamza and tashdid (diacritical marks) carry separate values?

Hamza is generally counted as equivalent to alif, and a letter carrying tashdid is typically counted only once rather than twice, though minor variations exist across different traditional schools.

The Historical Origins of the Abjad System

This ordering of letters is believed to trace back to ancient Phoenician and Aramaic scripts, where letters were traditionally assigned numerical values according to their inherited sequence. This system was already in use in Arabic before Islam, and was later adapted for various scholarly and spiritual purposes, including Ilm-e-Jafar. This system remains in use today across Arabic, Persian, and Urdu-speaking regions, particularly for recording historical dates through poetic chronograms, in which a meaningful phrase's combined abjad value encodes a specific year.

How Abjad Calculations Are Used in Practice

Once a name or phrase's abjad value is calculated, this number becomes the raw material for further analysis in various branches of Ilm-e-Jafar. In Ilm-e-Jafar Aasar, this total is combined with other figures and divided by a specific number to determine an interpretation. In the search for Ism-e-Azam, a person's name value is compared against the values of Allah's beautiful names. In name compatibility analysis, two individuals' values are compared against each other. In every case, the same underlying abjad table serves as the common foundation.

A Worked Example

Consider the name "Zainab," made up of the letters Zay, Ya, Noon, Ba. Their respective abjad values are 7, 10, 50, and 2, which together sum to 69. This total of 69 becomes the basis for further calculations — whether comparing it against the values of Allah's beautiful names to search for a suited Ism-e-Azam, or combining it with other figures in an Ilm-e-Jafar Aasar calculation to reach a final interpretive result.

Why Precision Matters

Because every letter carries an exact, fixed value, precision in transcription matters a great deal. A single misspelled letter, or the accidental inclusion of a diacritical mark that shouldn't be counted, can shift the entire calculation and produce a different — and therefore inaccurate — result. This is one reason traditional practitioners were trained carefully in Arabic orthography before being entrusted with these calculations, and why modern digital tools are designed to handle letter separation and counting with careful, consistent rules.

Abjad Beyond Ilm-e-Jafar

It's worth noting that the abjad system has uses well beyond Ilm-e-Jafar itself. It has historically been used in Islamic numerology more broadly, in the composition of poetic chronograms that encode a year within a meaningful phrase, and even in some traditional numbering systems for book chapters and Quranic verses in older manuscripts. This wider historical usage underscores that abjad is not a narrow or obscure system invented solely for spiritual guidance, but a broadly applied traditional numerical framework with centuries of documented use across the Arabic-speaking and Persian-influenced world.

Learning to Read the Table

For readers interested in trying a calculation themselves, a good starting exercise is to take their own first name, look up each letter's traditional Arabic equivalent, sum the corresponding abjad values, and see what total emerges. This simple exercise offers a hands-on introduction to a system that underlies every single tool within Ilm-e-Jafar, from the simplest question to the most detailed twenty-eight-letter analysis, and it is often the first step practitioners take when beginning to learn this tradition seriously.

A Final Thought

The abjad table may look, at first glance, like a simple list of letters and numbers. Yet within this ancient framework lies centuries of scholarly effort, spiritual reflection, and cultural transmission, all quietly encoded into twenty-eight letters and their assigned values.

Passing the Knowledge Forward

Just as this table has been copied, memorized, and transmitted from teacher to student for well over a thousand years, today's digital platforms carry that same responsibility forward — presenting the same values, the same traditional order, and the same careful attention to accuracy that has always defined this system, so that a tradition this old can continue to be understood correctly by a new generation of learners.

Conclusion

The abjad system is the foundation of Ilm-e-Jafar, and without understanding it, grasping the depth of this practice is not possible. This ancient numerical ordering of letters has been used for centuries for spiritual guidance, name compatibility, and various forms of traditional analysis. Today, modern tools perform all of this calculation automatically and instantly, but its traditional foundation remains exactly as firmly rooted as it was centuries ago, connecting today's user to a very long and well-documented chain of practice.
