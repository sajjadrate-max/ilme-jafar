The Method of Ilm-e-Jafar Asar

Ilm-e-Jafar Asar is the second major branch of Ilm-e-Jafar, based primarily on numerical calculation. This method takes four elements into account: the questioner's name, their mother's name, the day the question is asked, and the letters of the question itself. In this article we explore the complete calculation of Ilm-e-Jafar Asar, the principles behind it, and an accessible way to understand it.

The Meaning of "Asar" and the Basis of This Method

The word "Asar" means traces or effects. This method is based on the idea that a specific numerical energy or "trace" is hidden within a person's name, their mother's name, and the day the question is asked, which can be understood through Abjad calculation. The mother's name is included specifically because maternal lineage is traditionally considered more certain and closely established, whereas paternal lineage was, in some traditional contexts, considered to carry a degree of uncertainty.

The Complete Calculation, Step by Step

Step 1: The questioner's full name is written and its Abjad value calculated.

Step 2: The mother's name is written and its Abjad value calculated in the same way.

Step 3: The day of the week on which the question is asked is assigned a specific traditional numeric value (each day traditionally carries its own fixed value, such as Sunday, Monday, and so on).

Step 4: The Abjad value of all the letters in the question itself is also calculated.

Step 5: These four values (name + mother's name + day + question) are added together.

Step 6: The resulting total is divided by 4, and the remainder — 0, 1, 2, or 3 — determines the nature of the result.

Why the Number 4?

The choice of the number 4 is traditionally associated with the four classical elements — fire, water, air, and earth. Traditional scholars believed that everything in creation arises from a combination of these four basic elements, which is why this number is also used as the basis for the calculation, so that the result points toward one of these four fundamental natures.

The Traditional Meanings of the Remainders

Traditionally, each remainder points to a particular nature:

Remainder 0 (complete division): Generally interpreted as pointing toward the matter being complete or fruitful.

Remainder 1 (fire-related influence): May point toward passion, urgency, or an emotional cause.

Remainder 2 (water-related influence): Understood to point toward gentleness, emotional matters, or an internal state.

Remainder 3 (air/earth-related influence): May point toward temporary difficulties, uncertainty, or an external cause.

These interpretations are recorded with slight variations across different traditional texts, and different practitioners interpret them according to their own particular tradition.

The Practical Benefit of Ilm-e-Jafar Asar

This method is considered particularly useful for questions of a personal nature — such as those related to health, relationships, business, or domestic matters. Since it incorporates the person's own name and their mother's name, it is regarded as a more personalized analysis compared to Ilm-e-Jafar Akhbar, which relies solely on the letters of the question.

A Practical Example

Suppose the questioner's name is "Ahmad," whose Abjad value is: Alif=1, Ha=8, Meem=40, Dal=4 — total 53. The mother's name is "Fatima," whose value is: Fa=80, Alif=1, Ta=9, Meem=40, Ha=5 — total 135. The question is asked on a Wednesday, with a traditional value (for example) of 4. Suppose the question's letters total 62. Adding these four: 53+135+4+62=254. Dividing by 4: 254÷4=63 remainder 2. So the remainder is 2, which traditionally points toward a water-related influence. This example illustrates how the calculation is completed, though in actual practice the day and letter values are drawn from specific traditional tables.

Variations Across Traditional Texts

It is worth noting that different traditional manuscripts and texts on Ilm-e-Jafar Asar show minor variations in the values assigned to days and in the interpretation of remainders. Some traditions give Monday greater significance, others give it to Friday. Similarly, the interpretation of remainders differs slightly among practitioners based on their particular training and tradition. Remember — Important Advice

Ilm-e-Jafar Asar is also simply a traditional means of guidance. In serious medical, psychological, or urgent matters, consulting relevant professionals is essential, and for spiritual matters, consulting a knowledgeable and trustworthy scholar is advisable. Rather than relying entirely on the result, it should be combined with prayer, patience, and trust in Allah.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What if the mother's name is not known?
A: In such cases, a commonly used name or guardian's name may traditionally be used, though it is better to determine the correct name to ensure a more accurate result.

Q: Does changing the day change the result?
A: Yes, since the day's traditional numeric value is included in the calculation, the same question asked on different days can produce different results.

Combining Both Ilm-e-Jafar Methods for Deeper Insight

Some experienced practitioners choose to perform both Akhbar and Asar calculations for the same question, comparing the results to see whether they point in a similar direction. While this is not a requirement, it is sometimes used as an additional way of gaining confidence in the traditional guidance received, particularly for especially significant or difficult questions.

Respecting the Limits of This Traditional Method

It bears repeating that this numerical method, however structured and precise its calculations may appear, remains a traditional cultural practice rather than a scientifically validated system. Approaching it with the right balance of interest and humility — appreciating it as part of a rich heritage while keeping ultimate trust in Allah — is the healthiest way to engage with this tradition.

Preserving the Integrity of the Tradition

As this traditional method continues to be practiced through modern digital platforms, maintaining fidelity to the original calculation principles, rather than introducing arbitrary modifications for convenience, helps ensure that the results generated remain consistent with the centuries-old tradition from which this practice originates.

The Continuing Appeal of Numerical Traditions

Numerical and letter-based traditions like this one continue to hold appeal for many people even in the modern era, offering a structured, systematic approach to seeking guidance that feels tangible and concrete compared to more abstract forms of contemplation, though this appeal should never overshadow the primacy of direct worship and supplication to Allah.
