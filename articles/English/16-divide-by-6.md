# Divide by 6 — Understanding the Likelihood of Help or Support

Many of life's challenges become significantly easier when we have the support of others. Dividing by 6 within Ilm-e-Jafar Aasar offers a traditional way to gauge the likelihood of receiving help or cooperation from others in a given matter. This article explores this method in detail.

## The Method

As with other Aasar calculations, the abjad values of the questioner's name, their mother's name, the day, and the question are summed together. This total is divided by 6, and the remainder indicates the traditional likelihood category for receiving support.

## What the Result Reveals

This division's core purpose is to indicate whether others are likely to offer meaningful support and cooperation, or whether the questioner should prepare to rely more heavily on their own effort and resourcefulness. This isn't a judgment of anyone's character, but a traditional reading of the current circumstances surrounding a particular matter.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can this tell me exactly who will help me?**
No, it only indicates the general likelihood of receiving support, not the identity of any specific person.

**If the result suggests low likelihood of help, should I stop asking others for support?**
Not necessarily. It's still appropriate to seek help; the result simply suggests that more patience and independent effort may be needed alongside that request.

**Is this method used only for business partnerships?**
No, it applies to family matters, educational pursuits, or any situation where cooperation from others could play a role.

## Balancing Cooperation and Self-Reliance

Islamic teaching values both cooperation and self-reliance. On one hand, the Quran encourages mutual assistance in matters of righteousness and piety (Surah Al-Ma'idah, 5:2). On the other hand, individuals are also encouraged to rely on their own effort and initiative. The result of dividing by 6 essentially helps a questioner understand which of these two principles deserves greater emphasis in their current circumstances, so they neither depend excessively on others nor feel entirely isolated when support might genuinely be available.

## A Worked Example

Suppose someone starting a new venture asks: "Will I find a business partner?" Once the relevant abjad values are summed and divided by 6, a favorable result would encourage the questioner to reach out to their network and discuss partnership possibilities, since circumstances appear supportive. An unfavorable result would suggest starting on a smaller scale independently for now, with the possibility of revisiting partnership later once conditions become more favorable.

## Receiving Help With Humility

When results do point toward available help, it's worth remembering the importance of receiving that help with grace and humility rather than pride or reluctance. Islamic teaching does not view accepting assistance in a time of genuine need as a weakness but as a natural, acceptable part of human life. A person who receives help graciously, and who later extends similar help to others, participates in the same cycle of mutual support that Islamic teaching consistently encourages throughout the Quran and Hadith.

## The Role of Social Bonds

Human beings are inherently social, and many successful endeavors are, at their core, the product of collective effort. The result of this division also serves as a gentle reminder of how important it is to nurture one's relationships and social bonds — since these relationships often become the very source of support when times get difficult. Those who neglect their relationships during good times sometimes find themselves without a support network when they need it most, which is why maintaining good relations consistently, not only during moments of need, is traditionally emphasized.

## Frequently Asked Questions (Continued)

**Can family relationships specifically be assessed with this method?**
Yes, this method applies equally well to questions about support from family members, friends, colleagues, or any social relationship relevant to the matter at hand.

**Does a favorable result guarantee help will actually be given?**
No guarantee exists in any traditional method — it indicates a favorable likelihood based on traditional calculation, not a certainty, and the questioner should still take reasonable, proactive steps to seek that support.

## Cultivating a Support Network Proactively

Beyond simply assessing the current likelihood of help, this division can also serve as a prompt for proactive relationship-building. If a result suggests limited support is currently available, this can be understood not as a permanent verdict, but as an invitation to invest more deliberately in relationships over time — reaching out to old friends, strengthening family ties, and being genuinely present and helpful to others, so that when the questioner later faces difficulty, a wider circle of goodwill and mutual obligation naturally exists to draw upon.

## A Final Reflection on Reciprocity

One of the deeper lessons embedded in this division is the principle of reciprocity — that a person who has received generous help during difficult times bears a natural responsibility to extend similar generosity to others when their own circumstances improve. This cyclical understanding of community support reflects a core value found throughout Islamic ethics, in which individual wellbeing and communal responsibility are never viewed as separate concerns, but as two sides of the very same coin.

## When Support Doesn't Materialize

Sometimes, despite a favorable result and genuine effort to seek help, support simply doesn't come through as hoped. In such moments, it's important not to interpret this as personal rejection or failure, but rather as an invitation to explore alternative paths, seek support from a different source, or discover unexpected inner resourcefulness that might otherwise have remained untapped. Many people later look back on periods of unexpected self-reliance as some of the most formative and growth-filled seasons of their lives.

## A Closing Thought on Community

Ultimately, this division reflects a broader truth about human life: none of us succeed entirely alone, and none of us are entirely without resources either. The wisdom lies in discerning, honestly and humbly, which balance of self-reliance and community support best fits our current moment.

## Practical Steps to Take Alongside This Guidance

Regardless of what the result indicates, a few practical steps remain universally useful: communicate your needs clearly to those who might help, express genuine gratitude for any support received, follow up with sincere thanks after a favor is done, and remain willing to reciprocate when others are later in need. These simple habits, practiced consistently, tend to strengthen a person's support network far more effectively than any single calculation ever could.

## One Last Note

Community and self-reliance are not opposites but genuine partners, each strengthening the other when balanced wisely — a lesson this simple traditional division quietly encodes within its structure, and one worth carrying forward into every area of life, not only the specific matter originally asked about.

## Conclusion

Dividing by 6 offers useful insight into the likelihood of receiving help or cooperation in a given matter. It helps a questioner strike the right balance between seeking support from others and relying on their own effort, while always remembering to act with dignity, gratitude, and continued trust in Allah, regardless of whether support comes from people or must be found within oneself, since Allah alone is the ultimate source of every provision and every helping hand extended along the way, whatever form that help ultimately takes.
