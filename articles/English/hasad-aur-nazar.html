The Signs of Envy and Its Spiritual Remedy

Envy (hasad) is an emotion in which a person, upon seeing another's blessing, wishes for its decline. Islam regards envy as a highly reprehensible trait. In this article we explore the reality of envy, its signs, its harms, and the recommended supplications and methods for guarding against it.

The Religious Standing of Envy

The final verse of Surah Al-Falaq specifically seeks refuge from the evil of the envier, which is evidence that envy carries a real and harmful spiritual effect. The Prophet ﷺ said that envy consumes good deeds just as fire consumes wood. This hadith shows that envy is not only harmful to others but severely damages the envious person's own deeds and faith.

The Difference Between Envy and Wholesome Aspiration

Islamic teaching draws a clear distinction between envy (hasad) and "ghibtah" (wholesome aspiration). In envy, a person wishes for the decline of another's blessing, whereas in ghibtah, a person, upon seeing another's blessing, wishes for the same blessing for themselves without wanting the other's blessing to end. Ghibtah is permitted only in two matters, according to hadith: knowledge and wealth, provided they are used for good. In other words, wishing "I hope I too could have such knowledge or wealth so that I could also do good with it" upon seeing a scholar or a generous person is entirely permissible and commendable.

Signs of Envy

Feeling uneasy at others' happiness and success, speaking ill of them, and internally wishing for their loss are common signs of envy. Beyond this, feeling a pang of jealousy upon hearing of someone's success, finding their praise unpleasant to hear, and searching for their faults without reason are also among the signs of envy. If these feelings are repeatedly experienced, one should examine one's own heart.

The Worldly and Spiritual Harms of Envy

A person who envies first harms themselves — they remain perpetually restless and dissatisfied, since there is always someone in the world who appears more fortunate or successful. This trait poisons a person's heart and destroys their good deeds. On a social level, envy also causes rifts in relationships, and throughout history, many major conflicts and disputes have originated from envy, as described in the Quran in the account of the sons of Prophet Adam (peace be upon him).

Recommended Supplications for Protection from Envy

Surah Al-Falaq specifically seeks refuge from the evil of the envier. Beyond this, Ayat al-Kursi and the recommended morning and evening remembrances are also considered means of protection from envy and the evil eye. The Prophet ﷺ used to seek protection for Hasan and Husayn through the Mu'awwidhatayn. Making these supplications a regular morning and evening practice is considered an effective spiritual means of protection against the negative effects of envy.

Practical Measures to Guard Against Envy

To keep one's heart free of envy, one is taught to feel happy at others' blessings and to pray for them. This practice not only brings spiritual peace but also strengthens social relationships. Scholars recommend that whenever a feeling of envy toward someone arises in the heart, one should immediately pray for that person from the heart — this practice softens the heart and weakens the feeling of envy.

The Role of Gratitude and Contentment

Another effective way of guarding against envy is remaining grateful for one's own blessings and practicing contentment. When a person focuses on the blessings present in their own life rather than dwelling on others' blessings, gratitude and contentment take the place of envy in their heart. The Prophet ﷺ also advised looking toward those less fortunate in worldly matters, so that one recognizes the value of Allah's blessings upon them.

Dealing with an Envious Person

If someone senses that another person feels envy toward them, it is traditionally recommended to avoid unnecessary display of one's blessings and to adopt an attitude of gentleness and good treatment toward that person, since the Prophet ﷺ taught patience and good character even toward an envious person, rather than seeking retaliation.

Envy Within Families and Close Relationships

Envy often proves most damaging when it occurs between siblings, close relatives, or lifelong friends, since these relationships typically involve frequent comparison and shared history. Recognizing and addressing envy within these close bonds early, through open communication and conscious effort to celebrate rather than resent each other's successes, can prevent lasting damage to relationships that are meant to be sources of mutual support.

Cultivating a Mindset of Abundance

Adopting a mindset that views blessings as abundant rather than scarce — recognizing that another person's success does not diminish one's own potential for blessing — helps naturally reduce the competitive comparison that often fuels envy. This shift in perspective, cultivated through regular reflection on Allah's limitless capacity to bless multiple people simultaneously, can transform how a person instinctively reacts to witnessing others' good fortune.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is a fleeting thought of envy in the heart a sin?
A: No, a sudden thought that arises in the heart without being acted upon is not counted as sin. Sin occurs when a person deliberately nurtures or acts upon that feeling.

Q: Is the evil eye always caused by envy?
A: Most traditions attribute the evil eye primarily to envy, though sometimes intense admiration or fascination, without any ill intent, can also cause it.

Overcoming Envy Through Self-Reflection

Regular self-examination helps a person recognize the early stirrings of envy before they take root. Taking a moment each day to reflect on one's own blessings and to check whether feelings of envy have crept into the heart toward anyone allows a person to address the issue early through prayer and seeking forgiveness, rather than letting it grow into a persistent negative trait.

The Long-Term Cost of Harboring Envy

Beyond its immediate spiritual harm, envy that is allowed to persist over years can permanently damage relationships, erode a person's sense of contentment, and create a cycle of bitterness that affects every aspect of life. Recognizing this long-term cost can serve as strong motivation to actively work against this trait rather than allowing it to become an ingrained habit of the heart.

Turning Envy into Motivation for Good

Rather than allowing feelings of envy to fester, redirecting that energy toward permissible ghibtah — using another's success as motivation to work honestly toward similar achievements through one's own effort and prayer — transforms a potentially destructive emotion into a constructive source of personal growth and ambition.
