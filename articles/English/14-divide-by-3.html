Divide by 3 — Understanding What Stage a Matter Is At

Among the various traditional divisions in Ilm-e-Jafar Aasar, dividing by 3 addresses a very natural human question: not what a problem is, but where it currently stands. Is it just beginning, somewhere in the middle, or nearing its conclusion? This article explores this method in depth.

The Method

As with other Aasar calculations, the abjad values of the questioner's name, their mother's name, the day, and the question are summed together. This total is divided by 3, and the remainder — 0, 1, or 2 — indicates which of three traditional stages the matter has reached.

What the Three Stages Represent

The three traditional stages broadly correspond to beginning, middle, and near completion. A result pointing to the beginning stage suggests the matter is still in its early phase, requiring patience and continued groundwork. A result pointing to the middle stage suggests the matter is progressing but not yet resolved, often the most challenging phase where persistence matters most. A result pointing to near completion suggests the matter is approaching resolution, whatever form that resolution ultimately takes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can this method tell me exactly how long until the matter concludes?

No, it only indicates the current stage, not a specific timeframe. For timing-related questions, dividing by 7 is traditionally considered more suitable.

If the result shows the matter is just beginning, should effort continue?

Absolutely. An early stage doesn't mean failure — it simply indicates that reaching a resolution will take more time and continued effort, so patience and persistence remain essential.

Is this method limited to business matters only?

No, it applies to any kind of situation — a relationship, an educational pursuit, a medical treatment, or any personal project where the questioner wants to understand where things currently stand.

The Three Stages and Human Psychology

Interestingly, psychologically speaking, people tend to experience any project or matter in three broad phases as well — the enthusiasm of a fresh start, the fatigue and difficulty of the middle stretch, and the hope or relief that comes as an ending approaches. The result of dividing by 3 essentially helps a questioner understand which of these psychological phases they are currently in, which can itself be reassuring. Recognizing that a stretch of frustration in the middle phase is a normal, temporary part of the process — not a sign of failure — can offer real comfort and encouragement to keep going.

Insights From Experienced Practitioners

Experienced practitioners often note that dividing by 3 proves especially helpful in situations where a person fears that a matter has "stalled" or "failed," when in reality it is simply progressing at its own natural pace. In such cases, this division provides a clear, reassuring frame of reference, helping to prevent unnecessary anxiety and hasty decisions made out of impatience rather than genuine need.

A Worked Example

Suppose someone starting a new business asks: "How is my business progressing?" Once the relevant abjad values are summed and divided by 3, a remainder pointing to the middle stage would suggest that the business is neither an immediate success nor a failure, but is progressing through a normal, often challenging developmental phase. The appropriate guidance here would be to continue steady effort, remain patient through the natural difficulties of this middle stage, and trust that persistence during this phase is what eventually leads toward the completion stage.

Appropriate Action for Each Stage

Different actions are traditionally recommended depending on which stage a result indicates. For an early-stage result, the focus should be on careful planning, sincere prayer, and building a solid foundation. For a middle-stage result, maintaining consistency and resisting the temptation to give up is considered most important, since this is typically the stage where people most often lose heart. For a near-completion result, gratitude and thoughtful decision-making about next steps become the priority, regardless of whether the outcome matches the original hope exactly.

The Number Three Across Traditions

It is worth noting that the number three carries symbolic weight across many traditional systems of thought, quite apart from Ilm-e-Jafar itself. Storytelling traditions across cultures often use a three-part structure — setup, conflict, and resolution. Agricultural traditions have long recognized three phases of a growing season — planting, tending, and harvest. These parallels suggest that the three-stage framework used here draws on a deeply natural and intuitive human way of understanding process and progression, rather than being an arbitrary or purely invented scheme.

When to Reassess

Since circumstances change over time, it's entirely reasonable to revisit this calculation for a genuinely evolved situation after a meaningful interval — typically weeks or months rather than days. Reassessing too frequently, however, tends to reflect anxiety rather than genuine need for updated guidance, and traditional practice discourages this kind of repeated, impatient questioning. A helpful rule of thumb is to only revisit a question once real, observable changes have occurred in the underlying situation, rather than simply because uncertainty feels uncomfortable in the moment.

A Worked Comparison

It can help to compare two hypothetical questioners using this method. One asks about a two-week-old business venture and receives a beginning-stage result — entirely expected and unremarkable, simply confirming the obvious. Another asks about a five-year relationship and receives the same beginning-stage result — a more striking finding, potentially suggesting that despite the passage of time, the relationship has not yet developed the depth or stability one might expect, warranting closer reflection on what has been holding it back.

Bringing It All Together

Ultimately, this method's greatest value may lie not in predicting an outcome, but in helping a person locate themselves honestly within their own journey — recognizing where they truly stand, rather than where fear or wishful thinking might otherwise place them.

Approaching Each Stage With the Right Mindset

Ultimately, the value of understanding one's stage is less about certainty and more about mindset. A person who knows they are in a beginning stage can approach setbacks with the patience appropriate to new ventures. A person who recognizes a middle stage can brace for the natural difficulty of that phase without mistaking it for failure. And a person nearing completion can prepare themselves emotionally and practically for what comes next, whatever specific form that outcome ultimately takes.

Conclusion

Dividing by 3 offers a simple and useful way to understand what stage a matter has reached — beginning, middle, or nearing completion. This method helps a questioner manage their expectations and emotions realistically, encouraging patience where needed and appropriate action at each stage, while always trusting that Allah's timing is ultimately the wisest timing of all, even when it does not match our own hopes and expectations. Whatever stage a result points to, it's worth remembering that stages are not fixed forever — with continued effort and prayer, a matter naturally moves from one stage toward the next, offering a helpful, momentary snapshot of an ongoing, evolving journey rather than a permanent verdict, and reminding every sincere and patient seeker that patience itself is a form of worship in its own right, valuable regardless of the eventual outcome.
