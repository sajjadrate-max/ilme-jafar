The Virtues of Friday and Recommended Practices

Friday holds a special and blessed status in Islam and is described as "Sayyid al-Ayyam," the master of days. Hadith describes countless virtues and characteristics of this day. In this article we explore the importance of Friday, its recommended practices, and the special hour for the acceptance of supplication.

The Special Importance of Friday

The Prophet ﷺ said the best day on which the sun rises is Friday. Prophet Adam (peace be upon him) was created on this day, was admitted to Paradise on this day, and was removed from Paradise on this day as well. All these events reflect the extraordinary significance of Friday, which is why Muslims are encouraged toward special acts of worship on this day.

The Obligation of Friday Prayer

Friday prayer is obligatory upon adult, sane, resident men. In Surah Al-Jumu'ah, Allah states, "O you who believe, when the call is made for prayer on Friday, hasten to the remembrance of Allah and leave off trade." This verse establishes the importance and priority of Friday prayer. It is not obligatory upon women, travelers, or the sick, though they may perform it if they wish.

Recommended Practices in Preparation for Friday

Several recommended practices are described for Friday: taking a bath, wearing clean and good clothes, applying perfume, using the miswak, going early to the mosque, and listening silently to the sermon. The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever bathes on Friday, goes early to the mosque, walks there, sits close to the imam, and listens silently to the sermon, is rewarded with the equivalent of a year's fasting and standing in prayer for every step taken.

Reciting Surah Al-Kahf

Reciting Surah Al-Kahf on Friday is particularly recommended. In a hadith, the Prophet ﷺ said that whoever recites Surah Al-Kahf on Friday will have a light shine for them between the two Fridays. This chapter also mentions protection from the trial of the Dajjal (Antichrist), which is why it is especially recommended to recite on this day.

Abundant Blessings Upon the Prophet

Reciting abundant blessings upon the Prophet ﷺ on Friday and the night before it is also considered especially recommended. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Send abundant blessings upon me on Friday, for your blessings are presented to me." This is why many Muslims specifically dedicate Friday to reciting blessings upon the Prophet.

The Special Hour for the Acceptance of Supplication

Hadith mentions a special hour on Friday in which any supplication made is not rejected. Although the exact time of this hour is not clearly specified, most narrations point to the period between the afternoon prayer (Asr) and sunset, or during the sermon. This is why Muslims are encouraged to remain engaged in supplication and remembrance throughout this time in order to catch this special hour.

The Virtue of Charity on Friday

Many scholars consider giving charity on Friday more virtuous than on other days, since it is the best day of the week. Similarly, performing any good deed on this day — such as Quranic recitation, remembrance, or visiting relatives — is also considered to carry special reward.

Etiquette and Precautions on Friday

Remaining completely silent during the sermon and refraining entirely from conversation is obligatory, to the extent that even telling someone else to be quiet during the sermon is considered inappropriate. The Prophet ﷺ said that if you even tell someone to be quiet during the sermon, you have engaged in idle talk. This hadith reflects the seriousness and importance of attentiveness during the sermon.

Preparing Mentally for Friday Beyond Physical Preparation

Beyond the physical acts of bathing and dressing well, mentally preparing for Friday by setting an intention to focus fully during the sermon and prayer, and arriving with an open heart ready to absorb its lessons, enhances the spiritual benefit one derives from the entire experience of the day.

The Historical Origins of Friday's Significance

Scholars note that Friday was chosen by Allah as a distinct day of gathering for the Muslim community, separate from the sabbath observed by earlier religious communities, symbolizing a fresh beginning and a unique identity for the followers of the final revelation, while still honoring the shared tradition of setting aside a weekly day for communal worship.

Friday's Role in Personal Accountability

Many scholars encourage using the weekly rhythm of Friday as a natural checkpoint for personal accountability, reviewing one's actions and spiritual progress from the previous week and setting fresh intentions before the sermon begins, transforming what might otherwise be a routine weekly obligation into a genuine opportunity for ongoing self-improvement.

Extending Friday's Blessings Into the Following Week

The heightened spiritual atmosphere of Friday can serve as motivation to carry increased devotion, charity, and good conduct into the days that follow, rather than allowing the special observances of the day to remain isolated from the rest of one's weekly routine, ensuring that its benefits ripple outward through the entire week.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is Friday prayer obligatory for women?
A: No, Friday prayer is not obligatory upon women, though they may pray the regular Dhuhr prayer at the mosque or at home if they choose.

Q: What should be done if one is unable to attend Friday prayer for some reason?
A: In such a case, the regular Dhuhr prayer is performed, which takes the place of the Friday prayer.

Friday as a Weekly Spiritual Reset

Many Muslims treat Friday as an opportunity for a weekly spiritual reset, using the day to reflect on the past week's actions, renew their intentions, and recommit to their religious and personal goals. This weekly rhythm of reflection, combined with the special acts of worship recommended for the day, helps maintain consistent spiritual growth throughout the year.

Friday Gatherings and Community Bonds

Beyond its religious significance, Friday prayer also serves as a weekly occasion for the Muslim community to gather, strengthening social bonds and providing an opportunity for neighbors and acquaintances to reconnect, exchange greetings, and support one another, reinforcing the broader sense of community that Islam places great emphasis on.

Friday as an Opportunity for Renewed Devotion

Each Friday offers a fresh opportunity to deepen one's devotion, regardless of how the previous week unfolded, allowing believers to approach their faith with renewed energy and commitment on a regular, structured basis rather than waiting for occasional moments of inspiration that may arise unpredictably throughout the year.
