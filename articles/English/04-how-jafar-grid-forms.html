How the Jafar Grid Is Formed

The entire system of Ilm-e-Jafar Akhbar rests on a special table known as the "Jafar grid." This grid is the mechanism through which the letters of a question are rearranged into a new order, from which specific letters are then extracted. In this article, we'll look in detail at exactly how this grid is constructed and the logic behind it.

The Basic Structure

The Jafar grid is essentially a staircase-shaped table. Each word of the question is written on its own row, but each new row begins one column further to the right than the previous one — so the first word starts at the very first column, the second word starts one column over, the third word starts two columns over, and so on until the end. This produces a shape resembling a staircase.

Understanding Through an Example

Suppose the question is: "Will I get the job?" This contains five words: Will, I, get, the, job. When building the grid:

  • The first row ("Will") starts at column one
  • The second row ("I") starts at column two
  • The third row ("get") starts at column three
  • The fourth row ("the") starts at column four
  • The fifth row ("job") starts at column five

Wherever a word would begin before its designated starting column, empty spaces are left in those preceding cells.

Counting Column by Column

Once the grid is complete, all the letters are counted column by column — starting from the topmost cell in the first column, moving down to the bottom of that column, and then proceeding to the next column. This process completely rearranges the original order of the letters, producing an entirely new sequence. This new sequence becomes the foundation for the spiritual analysis that follows.

The Spiritual Significance of the Grid

According to traditional spiritual belief, just as the letters are given a new arrangement, the meanings hidden within them also emerge in a new form. This process is not viewed merely as a mathematical rearrangement but as a means of spiritual discovery, in which the sincerity and intention of the questioner also plays an important role.

How the Grid Is Built Today

In the past, this grid was drawn by hand on paper, requiring considerable time and skill. Today, with the help of modern technology, this process is completed automatically within moments. A user simply types their question, and the system separates the letters, arranges them into the staircase pattern, and instantly extracts the relevant letters to display.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the grid still be built if the question contains very few letters?

Yes, though a very short question may produce a limited grid, which can reduce the depth of the resulting analysis. It's generally recommended to phrase the question as a complete, natural sentence.

Does the grid depend on which language script is used?

Traditionally, the calculation is performed on Arabic letters, so questions written in Urdu or Arabic script naturally work within this system, since both draw on the same underlying alphabet.

How do practitioners avoid errors when building the grid by hand?

Traditionally trained practitioners follow strict, memorized rules for column alignment and letter counting, and cross-check their work carefully, since even a small misalignment can shift the entire resulting sequence.

Avoiding Common Mistakes

A few common errors can occur when constructing the grid, and it's worth being aware of them. Adding unnecessary diacritical marks or symbols to the question is one common mistake. Incorrectly separating words is another. Making the question excessively long or overly complex is a third, since this can cause the grid to become unwieldy and the resulting sequence harder to interpret cleanly. For this reason, it's generally advised to keep the question short, clear, and phrased in plain language.

The Grid and the Analysis That Follows

It's important to understand that the grid itself does not provide the answer directly — it is simply the mechanism through which specific letters are extracted. The actual spiritual analysis comes from combining the meaning of those extracted letters, their numerical value, and the context of the original question. This is why two different practitioners, even working from the same grid, might phrase their interpretation slightly differently, since interpretation also draws on a practitioner's training and traditional knowledge. Modern automated systems standardize this process, giving every user a consistent, uniform method regardless of who is asking.

Which Elements Are Excluded From the Grid

Not every character in a written question is included when building the grid. Punctuation marks such as question marks and commas are excluded, as are numerals and most diacritical marks. Only the core letters of each word are used. This is an important detail, because including extra symbols by mistake can shift the entire resulting sequence and produce an inaccurate extraction.

A Note on Different Grid Conventions

It's worth mentioning that slightly different conventions for building the Jafar grid exist across different regional and scholarly traditions. Some practitioners count letters top-to-bottom within each column, as described here, while others use a right-to-left convention across rows first. Some include the questioner's own name directly in the grid alongside the question, while others treat the name as a separate calculation entirely. This variation is not a flaw but a reflection of how a centuries-old oral and written tradition naturally develops small regional differences over time, much as different schools of Islamic jurisprudence differ on secondary matters while agreeing on core principles.

Practicing the Method by Hand

For those curious to understand the tradition more deeply, practicing the grid construction by hand — even just once — can be a valuable exercise. Take a short question, write out each word on its own staircase-aligned row, and then count the letters column by column to see the new sequence emerge. This hands-on exercise makes the underlying logic of the method far more intuitive than simply reading a description of it, and it connects the practitioner more directly to the centuries of scholars who performed this exact process by hand, long before any digital tool existed.

Final Thoughts

Whether built by hand on paper or generated instantly through a modern tool, the Jafar grid represents a beautiful intersection of language, mathematics, and spiritual tradition — a reminder that even the simplest-seeming letters can be arranged to reveal deeper layers of meaning, and that a centuries-old method can still find a natural home in the digital age without losing anything of its original character.

One Last Note

For readers who find this level of structural detail fascinating, it may help to know that many other traditional sciences share a similar staircase-based logic, reinforcing just how deeply woven letter arrangement is into centuries of spiritual and intellectual practice.

Conclusion

The Jafar grid is the heart of the entire Ilm-e-Jafar Akhbar process. This staircase-shaped arrangement gives the letters a new form, from which specific letters are extracted, and the meaning of those letters becomes the basis for answering the question. While this process is now completed instantly through automated tools, its underlying tradition remains exactly the same as it has been practiced for centuries, and understanding it is key to appreciating the depth, structure, and quiet elegance of this practice.
