A Complete Introduction to Abjad Numerals

Each of the 28 letters of the Arabic alphabet has been assigned a specific numeric value, known as the "Abjad" system. This system has been in use across the Islamic world for centuries and holds a foundational place in traditional sciences, particularly Ilm-e-Jafar. In this article we explore the history of the Abjad system, its complete arrangement, its traditional applications, and its central importance in Ilm-e-Jafar.

Origins and History of the Abjad System

The roots of the Abjad system trace back to ancient Semitic languages. Before Arabic, similar systems existed in Hebrew and Phoenician, where letters doubled as numbers. In Arabic, this system predates Islam and was originally used for commerce, record-keeping, and correspondence, since the modern numeral system (0-9) was not yet in common use.

Over time, especially during the Abbasid era when sciences and arts flourished, the Abjad system found deeper application in spiritual and scholarly fields. Sufi scholars and practitioners of traditional sciences adopted it as a way to understand the perceived spiritual significance of letters.

The Complete Arrangement of Abjad

The 28 Abjad letters are traditionally grouped into eight mnemonic words: Abjad, Hawwaz, Hutti, Kalaman, Sa'fas, Qarashat, Thakhaz, Dazagh. The values run as follows:

First group (units 1-9):
Alif=1, Ba=2, Jeem=3, Dal=4, Ha=5, Waw=6, Zay=7, Ha=8, Ta=9

Second group (tens 10-90):
Ya=10, Kaf=20, Lam=30, Meem=40, Noon=50, Seen=60, Ayn=70, Fa=80, Sad=90

Third group (hundreds 100-900):
Qaf=100, Ra=200, Sheen=300, Ta=400, Tha=500, Kha=600, Dhal=700, Dad=800, Za=900

Fourth letter:
Ghayn=1000

This ordering is known as the "Eastern Abjad," widely used across South Asia, Iran and Iraq. A separate "Western Abjad" ordering exists in parts of North Africa, with a slightly different arrangement of the final six letters. Since the South Asian tradition of Ilm-e-Jafar follows the Eastern Abjad, this website uses the same system.

How Letter Values Are Calculated

To find the Abjad value of a word or phrase, each letter's individual value is added together. For example, for the word "Ilm" (علم): Ayn=70, Lam=30, Meem=40 — summing gives 70+30+40=140. This same method can be applied to any name, question, or sentence to arrive at its total Abjad value. Diacritical marks are generally not counted, only the base letters.

Historical Chronograms — Abjad in Dating Events

Muslim civilization developed a fascinating tradition called "tareekhi mada" (historical chronogram), where the year of a significant event — the construction of a building, a king's coronation, or the completion of a book — was encoded within a meaningful Arabic or Persian phrase, such that the total Abjad value of the phrase's letters equaled that year. This art became so popular that many historical buildings, mosques, and book title pages carry such chronograms, which remain of interest to historians today. This demonstrates that the Abjad system was not merely a spiritual concept but a genuine literary and scholarly tradition.

Use in Spiritual and Scholarly Fields

The Abjad system has traditionally been applied across several spiritual sciences. Foremost among these is Ilm-e-Jafar, where the Abjad values of questions, names, and dates are extracted and analyzed. Beyond this, letter sciences, talismanic tables (wafq), and various regional traditions have also employed Abjad. Some traditions have discussed the Abjad values of certain Quranic verses, particularly the "muqatta'at" (disjointed letters) that open several chapters, such as Alif-Lam-Meem or Ya-Seen, though interpretations of these vary among commentators.

The Foundational Role of Abjad in Ilm-e-Jafar

Both branches of Ilm-e-Jafar — Akhbar and Asar — rely fundamentally on Abjad numerals. In Ilm-e-Jafar Akhbar, the letters of a question are arranged in a staircase grid based on their numeric order; in Ilm-e-Jafar Asar, the Abjad values of the questioner's name, mother's name, and question are summed and divided by 4. Neither method is possible without Abjad numerals, which is why understanding this system is considered a prerequisite for grasping Ilm-e-Jafar.

Abjad and Modern Science — An Important Clarification

It is important to understand that the Abjad system is an ancient, traditional, cultural framework — not an empirically proven scientific fact like modern mathematics. It should be viewed as part of the intellectual and spiritual heritage of Muslim civilization. Ilm-e-Jafar and related traditional methods are tools for guidance, not final verdicts. In every matter, ultimate trust and decision-making should rest with Allah.

Abjad Across Other Civilizations

Interestingly, the idea of linking letters to numbers is not unique to Arabic or Islamic tradition. Hebrew has a similar system called Gematria, long used in Jewish spiritual traditions. Ancient Greek also assigned numeric values to its letters, used by philosophers and mathematicians alike. This shows that the human fascination with the relationship between letters and numbers has been a widespread and ancient phenomenon across cultures, each with its own cultural and religious context.

A Practical Example — Calculating a Name's Abjad Value

Let's walk through a practical example. Suppose the name is "Kareem." Its letters are: Kaf=20, Ra=200, Ya=10, Meem=40. Adding these together: 20+200+10+40=270. This is the complete Abjad value of the name. The same method can be used to find the Abjad value of any name, title, or phrase, and this basic calculation forms the foundation for the more elaborate procedures used in Ilm-e-Jafar.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Are Abjad values the same across every language using the Arabic script?
A: Largely yes — since Urdu and Persian also use the Arabic script, shared letters carry the same value. However, additional Urdu letters (such as Ṭe, Ḍāl, Ṛe) don't have a distinct traditional Abjad value of their own and are generally treated as equivalent to their closest Arabic counterpart.

Q: Is there a difference between manual and automated calculation?
A: No, the underlying principles remain the same — automated calculation simply completes the process much faster.

Regional Variations in Abjad Interpretation

While the numeric values themselves are widely agreed upon within the Eastern Abjad tradition, the interpretation and application of these values in spiritual practice can vary from region to region and from one traditional text to another. Some manuscripts assign additional symbolic meanings to particular numbers, associating them with specific elements, planets, or spiritual qualities. This diversity reflects the organic, centuries-long development of the tradition across different Muslim societies, from the Middle East to South Asia.

Learning to Calculate Abjad Values Yourself

For those interested in understanding the system more deeply, learning the eight mnemonic groups by heart is the traditional starting point. Many students of this science historically memorized the groupings as a chant, much like learning a multiplication table, before progressing to more advanced calculations used in Ilm-e-Jafar. Today, digital tools have made this process far more accessible, though the underlying knowledge remains rooted in this centuries-old framework.

The Symbolic Value of Numbers in Traditional Thought

Beyond letter calculations, traditional scholars also attributed symbolic significance to certain numbers themselves, associating them with balance, completeness, or spiritual states. While such associations vary between texts and should not be treated as fixed rules, they reflect how deeply numerology was woven into the intellectual life of past scholars, philosophers, and mystics across the Muslim world.
