The Method of Ilm-e-Jafar Akhbar

Ilm-e-Jafar Akhbar is one of the two foundational branches of traditional Ilm-e-Jafar. In this method, the letters of a question are arranged in a specific sequence, from which an answer is derived. In this article we explore the complete methodology of Ilm-e-Jafar Akhbar, the principles behind it, and a practical way to understand it.

What Is Ilm-e-Jafar Akhbar?

The word "Akhbar" means news or information. This method is named as such because it is meant to provide the questioner with "news" or information related to their question. This approach is based purely on the arrangement and selection of letters, and does not involve the division of numbers (which is used in Ilm-e-Jafar Asar).

The Complete Method, Step by Step

Step 1 — Writing the Question: The questioner first writes their question clearly and concisely in Arabic or Urdu script. The clearer and more concise the question, the more reliable the result is traditionally considered to be.

Step 2 — Counting the Letters: All the letters of the question are counted individually, excluding spaces.

Step 3 — The Staircase Arrangement: These letters are arranged in a specific "staircase" grid pattern. This grid arranges letters in a particular geometric sequence, a method traditionally passed down through generations.

Step 4 — Selecting Every Fourth Letter: Every fourth letter is extracted from this grid. The number 4 is traditionally associated with the four classical elements (fire, water, air, earth), which hold a foundational place in the division of letters.

Step 5 — Forming a New Set: These extracted letters together form a new set of letters, called the "Jafar result."

The Spiritual Nature of Letters and the Four Elements

In traditional Ilm-e-Jafar, each Arabic letter is associated with one of four basic elements: fire, water, air, or earth. According to some traditions, letters such as Alif, Ha, Ta, Meem, Fa, Sheen, and Dhal are associated with fire, while other letters are attributed to the remaining elements. Each element is believed to carry a specific spiritual nature and influence — fire-associated letters with passion and speed, water-associated letters with gentleness and calm, air-associated letters with rapid change, and earth-associated letters with stability and steadiness.

How the Result Is Understood

The resulting set of letters is analyzed according to traditional principles. Each letter is given a traditional spiritual disposition (positive, negative, or neutral), and the overall answer to the question is derived from the collective tendency of these letters. If the majority of letters are positive, the outcome is generally considered encouraging; if negative letters dominate, caution and patience are advised; and if the letters are mixed, the result is understood as moderate or pointing to a temporary difficulty.

The Difference Between Ilm-e-Jafar Akhbar and Ilm-e-Jafar Asar

It's important to understand that Ilm-e-Jafar Akhbar and Ilm-e-Jafar Asar are two distinct methods. Akhbar derives its result purely from the arrangement of the question's letters, while Asar sums the Abjad values of the questioner's name, mother's name, and question, then divides by 4. Both methods hold their own traditional standing, and some practitioners compare both to arrive at a stronger conclusion.

Historical Background of the Staircase Grid

The concept of the staircase grid derives from ancient Arabic calligraphy and numerical arrangement traditions. Traditional practitioners once drew this grid by hand on paper, arranging letters from right to left, top to bottom. This process was considered to require great care and precision, since even a single misplaced letter could completely change the result. Today, modern technology completes this complex calculation automatically within seconds, though the underlying principles remain centuries old.

Etiquette of Writing a Question

Traditionally, it is recommended that a few points of etiquette be observed before writing a question — performing ablution (wudu), beginning with Bismillah, and writing the question in clear, unambiguous language. It's considered best to avoid vagueness or unnecessary detail in the question, since the number and arrangement of letters directly affects the outcome. Similarly, it's considered preferable to ask one specific question at a time rather than combining several questions together.

Caution and Important Advice

This is a traditional technique and should be used with seriousness and sincere intention. Rather than treating the result as a final decision, it should be taken as guidance, and important decisions should always include prayer, istikhara (seeking guidance through prayer), and consultation with knowledgeable people. Maintaining sincere intention, being in a state of ritual purity, and having complete trust in Allah are also traditionally recommended when asking a question.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can asking the same question repeatedly produce different results?
A: No, since the calculation is based on a fixed arrangement of letters, the result for the same question will always be the same.

Q: Does the language of the question (Urdu or Arabic) affect the result?
A: Yes, since the calculation is letter-based, a question written in a different language may produce different letters and thus a different result.

The Discipline Required for Accurate Practice

Historically, mastering this method required years of apprenticeship under an experienced teacher, since the precise arrangement of letters and correct application of the staircase grid demanded significant skill and attention to detail. Today's automated tools have made the calculation itself instantaneous, but the underlying knowledge of how and why the method works remains rooted in this same centuries-old tradition.

Respecting the Tradition While Using Modern Tools

Even as technology simplifies the calculation process, it remains important to approach the practice with the same seriousness, sincerity, and traditional etiquette that practitioners have observed for generations, rather than treating it as a casual or purely recreational exercise disconnected from its spiritual roots.

Understanding the Purpose Behind Traditional Rituals

Approaching any traditional practice with an understanding of its historical purpose and context, rather than treating it as an arbitrary set of rules, deepens appreciation for the cultural and intellectual heritage it represents, allowing modern practitioners to engage with it thoughtfully rather than mechanically.

A Closing Reflection on Tradition and Faith

Ultimately, engaging with traditional practices such as this one should always remain secondary to the core pillars of Islamic faith — prayer, sincere worship, and trust in Allah — serving as a supplementary source of traditional guidance rather than ever competing with or replacing these fundamental religious obligations.
