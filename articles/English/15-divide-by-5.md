# Divide by 5 — Understanding the Severity of a Matter

Once the basic nature of a problem is understood, a natural next question is how serious it actually is. Dividing by 5 within Ilm-e-Jafar Aasar addresses exactly this — offering a traditional way to gauge whether a matter is mild, moderate, or genuinely serious. This article explores this method in depth.

## The Method

As with other Aasar calculations, the abjad values of the questioner's name, their mother's name, the day, and the question are summed together. This total is divided by 5, and the remainder — a number between 0 and 4 — indicates the traditional severity category the matter falls into.

## What the Result Reveals

The purpose of this division is to help a questioner understand how serious their situation truly is — mild, moderate, or more serious — so they can respond with an appropriately calibrated level of concern and effort, rather than either overreacting to a minor issue or underestimating a genuinely serious one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can severity change over time?**
Yes, if circumstances or the question itself change, a new calculation can yield a different result. Life situations are rarely static, so periodically reassessing a genuinely evolved matter is reasonable.

**If the result indicates high severity, should I panic?**
No. A high-severity result is a signal to take the matter seriously and act with urgency, not a reason for panic. It should motivate focused, thoughtful action rather than fear.

**Should mild-severity matters be ignored?**
No, even mild matters deserve appropriate attention, since neglecting them consistently can allow them to worsen over time. Priorities can simply be set accordingly.

## The Practical Value of Severity Assessment

Many people either take minor life problems far too seriously, leading to unnecessary stress, or take serious matters too lightly, leading to neglect. The result from dividing by 5 helps correct this imbalance. When a questioner knows realistically how severe their situation is, they can allocate their time, energy, and resources more wisely — full and urgent attention for serious matters, and balanced, proportionate attention for lighter ones.

## Severity and Patience in Islamic Teaching

Islamic teaching encourages patience in the face of every trial, however small or large. A well-known hadith reminds believers that whatever hardship befalls a believer — even the prick of a thorn — Allah forgives some of their sins in exchange. This means that the purpose of assessing severity is not to induce fear, but to enable realistic, practical planning, while patience and trust in Allah remain essential at every level of severity, whether the trial is minor or significant.

## A Worked Example

Suppose someone asks: "How serious is my ongoing conflict with a family member?" Once the relevant abjad values are summed and divided by 5, a moderate-severity result would suggest the matter is neither trivial nor a serious emergency, and can likely be resolved through patient conversation, sincere apology where warranted, and continued effort — rather than requiring urgent, drastic intervention.

## Combining With Other Divisions

Dividing by 5 is frequently combined with division by 4, to first understand the nature of a problem, and then its severity. Together, these two calculations give a questioner a clearer, two-dimensional picture — both what the problem fundamentally is, and how urgently it needs to be addressed — allowing for a much more informed and proportionate response than either calculation could offer on its own.

## Severity and Emotional Wellbeing

It's worth noting that understanding the true severity of a matter can also carry genuine psychological benefit. Anxiety often grows not from a problem's actual scale, but from uncertainty about that scale — the mind tends to imagine worst-case scenarios when clarity is missing. A calm, structured assessment, even a traditional and symbolic one, can help interrupt this spiral of imagined catastrophe, replacing vague dread with a more concrete, manageable understanding of what one is actually facing. This psychological steadying effect may be one of the quieter benefits of this practice, alongside its more explicitly spiritual dimension.

## Avoiding Two Common Extremes

Two opposite errors are common when interpreting severity results. The first is dismissing a serious result too quickly, telling oneself "it's probably not that bad" without taking meaningful action. The second is catastrophizing a mild or moderate result, treating it as far more dire than indicated. Both patterns undermine the practical value of the assessment. The healthiest response is to take the result at face value, neither minimizing nor exaggerating it, and to plan a response proportionate to what it actually indicates.

## A Note on Cumulative Trials

It's also worth acknowledging that when multiple mild or moderate difficulties pile up at once, their cumulative emotional weight can feel far heavier than any single result might suggest. In such cases, addressing overall wellbeing — through rest, prayer, and support from loved ones — matters just as much as addressing each individual concern separately, since sustained stress from several smaller matters can sometimes be just as taxing as a single more severe one.

## When to Seek Additional Support

If a result consistently points toward high severity across multiple genuine reassessments, or if the underlying situation involves significant risk to health, finances, or safety, this is a clear signal to seek appropriate professional support alongside spiritual reflection — whether that means a doctor, a financial advisor, a lawyer, or a trusted counselor. Traditional spiritual guidance was never intended to stand alone in matters that call for specialized expertise, and recognizing this limitation is itself part of using the practice wisely and responsibly.

## A Final Word

Severity assessment, done sincerely and without excessive fear, is ultimately an exercise in clear-eyed self-awareness — helping a person see their circumstances as they truly are, rather than through the distorting lens of either anxiety or denial.

## Practicing Proportionate Response

Developing the habit of responding proportionately to life's difficulties — neither dismissing genuine concerns nor magnifying minor ones — is a skill that benefits from regular practice. Over time, a person who consistently uses tools like this thoughtfully may find themselves naturally developing better calibrated instincts about severity even without any formal calculation, simply through the habit of pausing to assess before reacting.

## Closing Thoughts

Whatever the severity assessment reveals, remember that every trial carries within it an opportunity for spiritual growth, deeper reliance on Allah, and, often, unexpected personal strength discovered only through facing difficulty directly and honestly rather than avoiding or minimizing it.

## A Practical Reminder for Daily Life

Keeping this balanced perspective close at hand during ordinary daily stress — not just major life crises — can gradually reshape how a person relates to difficulty altogether, replacing reactive worry with a steadier, more grounded response to whatever each day brings.

## Conclusion

Dividing by 5 offers a realistic and balanced way to assess how serious a matter truly is. This method helps a person allocate their time, energy, and resources wisely — full attention where genuinely needed, and appropriate but proportionate attention for lighter concerns — while patience and trust in Allah's decree remain essential throughout, whatever the assessed level of severity turns out to be, and whatever combination of trials a person may currently be facing, remembering always that ease follows hardship in due time, exactly as promised in the Quran, and that no trial is ever wasted in the sight of Allah.
