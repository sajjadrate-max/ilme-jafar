# Divide by 12 — Understanding the General Nature of a Matter

Sometimes what a person wants isn't a specific answer to a specific question, but a broader sense of the overall character of a situation. Dividing by 12 within Ilm-e-Jafar Aasar addresses this wider view, drawing on the traditional framework of the twelve zodiacal signs. This article explores this method in detail.

## The Method

As with other Aasar calculations, the abjad values of the questioner's name, their mother's name, the day, and the question are summed together. This total is divided by 12, and the remainder corresponds to one of twelve traditional categories describing the overall nature of the matter.

## The Twelve Traditional Categories

Each of the twelve traditional categories carries its own distinct character. Some are associated with leadership and new beginnings, others with stability and financial matters, others with communication and quick exchange, others with domestic and emotional matters, others with honor and visibility, others with careful, detail-oriented effort, others with balance and partnership, others with hidden or deep matters, others with travel and distant news, others with achievement and career advancement, others with fresh thinking and unexpected support, and the final category with spirituality and the acceptance of prayer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is this the same as Western astrology?**
No, although it borrows the traditional terminology of the twelve zodiacal signs as a symbolic framework, the calculation itself is based entirely on abjad numerology rather than the position of celestial bodies. From an Islamic perspective, the stars themselves hold no independent power — all authority belongs to Allah alone.

**Can different matters for the same person yield different categories?**
Yes, since each matter has its own distinct question and abjad value, different concerns for the same individual can naturally point toward entirely different categories.

**Does the result change over time?**
If the underlying question and circumstances change meaningfully, a new calculation can yield a different result, reflecting the natural evolution of a person's situation.

## Element Correspondence With Traditional Frameworks

It's interesting to note that this same twelve-part framework echoes across various traditional philosophical traditions, where the twelve months of the year were long linked to different qualities and effects. The concept of division by 12 within Ilm-e-Jafar appears to draw on this same broad traditional framework, adapted within an Islamic spiritual context to describe the character of everyday matters rather than to make astrological predictions.

## A Worked Example

Suppose someone asks: "What is the general nature of my marriage right now?" Once the relevant abjad values are summed and divided by 12, a result pointing toward balance and partnership would suggest that the relationship currently reflects a strong element of mutual understanding and cooperation, while a result pointing toward hidden or deep matters might suggest unresolved issues beneath the surface that deserve honest, open conversation between the couple.

## Stability Versus Change

Broadly speaking, the twelve results from this division can be grouped into those suggesting stability and those suggesting change. A result suggesting stability advises the questioner to remain steady on their current path. A result suggesting change may indicate that openness to new possibilities, rather than rigid attachment to familiar patterns, would serve the questioner better going forward.

## Frequently Asked Questions (Continued)

**Which other divisions pair well with this one?**
Dividing by 12 is often used alongside division by 4 (nature of the problem) and division by 9 (spiritual state), together forming a fuller, multi-dimensional picture of a matter.

**Is this division suitable for questions about business ventures?**
Yes, it works equally well for business matters, relationships, health concerns, or any broad life situation where an overall character reading would be valuable.

## Using This Division Wisely

Experienced users often describe this division as offering "the big picture" — helping a questioner step back from a single narrow concern to see the wider character of what they're dealing with. This makes it especially useful at the beginning of a new venture, relationship, or major decision, when understanding the general temperament of what lies ahead can help set appropriate expectations from the very start, before diving into the specific details that other, more targeted divisions can later address.

## New Users and This Division

For those unfamiliar with this method, a helpful way to approach it is to first ask a broad question — such as simply understanding the character of a new job or relationship — before moving on to more specific questions using other traditional divisions. This layered approach mirrors how a person might naturally get to know a new situation in real life, starting with a general impression before exploring specific details more closely over time.

## The Value of Broad Perspective Before Detail

There is a well-recognized wisdom in gaining a broad understanding of a situation before diving into its finer details, whether in traditional spiritual practice or in ordinary decision-making. A doctor examines overall symptoms before ordering specific tests; a teacher assesses a student's general level before addressing particular weaknesses. This division mirrors that same wise sequencing, offering a broad character assessment that naturally sets the stage for more detailed traditional inquiries that might follow.

## A Closing Thought

Understanding the overall temperament of a situation — before getting lost in its specific details — often brings a sense of clarity and calm that makes every subsequent decision feel more grounded and less overwhelming.

## Bringing the Categories to Life

To make the twelve categories more memorable, some practitioners describe them using simple everyday language rather than formal zodiacal terms — calling one "the starter," another "the builder," another "the connector," another "the nurturer," another "the leader," another "the perfectionist," another "the partner," another "the seeker," another "the traveler," another "the achiever," another "the innovator," and the final one "the believer." Whatever labels are used, the underlying purpose remains the same: capturing a distinct flavor of experience that colors how a particular matter tends to unfold over time.

## One Final Reminder

Whatever category a matter falls into, remember that these traditional labels describe general tendencies, not fixed destinies — a person's own sincere choices, prayers, and continued efforts always retain the power to shape how any situation ultimately unfolds.

## A Note on Regional Variation

As with many divisions within Ilm-e-Jafar, the specific interpretive details attached to each of the twelve categories can vary slightly between different regional and scholarly traditions, much as the broader tradition of Ilm-e-Jafar itself has always shown some natural regional diversity while sharing a common core framework. This variation should be seen not as inconsistency, but as a reflection of a living tradition that has been thoughtfully adapted across many generations and geographic contexts.

## Conclusion

Dividing by 12 helps a questioner understand the general character and mood of their matter — whether it is stable, changing, or connected to a relationship — through the lens of twelve traditional categories. This method is particularly useful for anyone seeking a wide-angle view of their situation, while, as always, remembering that the final decision and outcome rest with Allah alone, whose knowledge of what truly lies ahead surpasses any traditional symbolic system, however thoughtfully and carefully constructed that particular symbolic system may genuinely be.
